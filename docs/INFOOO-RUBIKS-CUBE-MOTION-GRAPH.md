# Infooo World 002 — Rubik’s Cube Motion Graph

**Status:** Publicly released 2026-09-25, approved by Khizar. Indexable, in the sitemap, and linked from the `/infooo/` hub — see the release entry below.

## Scope

This world teaches how legal face turns permute a real cube state, at three genuinely different sizes: 2×2, 3×3 (flagship, default) and 4×4. The local engine owns cubie identity, position, sticker orientation, visible facelets, sequences, inverse sequences, and both deterministic (test-only) and real random scrambles. Shared Infooo code only owns the shell, stage, accessibility primitives, and honest-source disclosure.

## Truth boundaries

- **Fact, per size** — 2×2: eight corner cubies, no edges, no fixed centers, 24 visible stickers. 3×3: eight corners, twelve edges, six fixed-reference centers, 54 visible stickers (unchanged flagship model). 4×4: eight corners, twenty-four movable edge-wing pieces, twenty-four movable center pieces (no single fixed center per face, unlike 3×3), 96 visible stickers. Every count follows the general formula 6×N² for stickers and is derived at runtime (`cubeCounts()`), never hardcoded in UI copy. A face turn is always a single outer-layer turn — no wide/slice moves are modeled at any size.
- **Model:** the Motion Graph groups the movable physical cubies by role — an outer ring of corners at every size; 3×3 adds a middle ring of twelve edges (unchanged); 4×4 instead adds a middle ring of twenty-four edge-wings and an inner ring of twenty-four centers, each grouped by which physical orbit (face or face-pair) the piece belongs to. A token keeps its colors and identity as it moves between position slots. The optional Advanced · Sticker View lays out the same state as 6×N² facelets, unfolded per face, and explicitly teaches that stickers are not independent physical pieces (8×3 + 12×2 + 6×1 = 54 at 3×3, etc). Neither visual is physical internal cube geometry.
- **Simulation:** "Randomize cube" generates a real scramble through legal moves only, from solved state, using `Math.random()` (never by inventing sticker colors) with a size-appropriate length and no immediate same-face repeats. "Watch it solve" replays the exact inverse of that scramble. This is a real, verified solution — restoring position and orientation exactly — but it is not presented as optimal, shortest, CFOP, or a general arbitrary-state solver; the on-screen copy says plainly that the solve reverses the generated scramble. The original curated seven-move flagship example (`oneTurnSequence`/`workingExampleSequence`) and manual face-turning were removed from the UI in the second feedback round (see below) to reduce surface complexity; the sequences remain as engine-level test fixtures.

## Turn · Trace · Understand

Kept as the core language, simplified after two rounds of direct feedback to cut surface complexity. **Turn**: Randomize and Watch it solve drive every move now — manual per-face turning was removed entirely (state, handlers and UI) per explicit feedback that it added complexity without teaching value beyond what Randomize/Solve already demonstrate. **Trace**: select one physical piece (sticker or motion-graph token) and follow it — a compact, always-visible strip states its colors and type plus a collapsing trail of every position visited, confirmed correct as-is by the user across both feedback rounds. **Understand**: step through a solve one move at a time, inspect every side via a free continuous camera, or open the Advanced Sticker View.

## Hidden movement and inspection

The cube view supports a genuine continuous 360° drag-to-orbit camera (mouse and touch, via Pointer Events) — added in the second feedback round to replace the original six-discrete-viewpoint-only model, which read as "just front/left/right" rather than a real 3D camera. Front, Right, Back, Left, Top and Bottom remain as one-tap/one-click jump-to presets (`presetAngle`); the header names whichever preset the current free angle is nearest to (`nearestViewpoint`), and a preset button only shows pressed when the camera is within 2° of it (`isNearPreset`) — so dragging away from every preset correctly shows none of them as active. All six presets together close the original gap where the yellow (Down-face) sticker could never be reached (root cause: every original viewpoint had a positive camera pitch, so the Down face's normal could never win the visibility dot-product test; Bottom mirrors Top's yaw at a negative pitch). A selected piece outside the visible faces gets its human-readable location and a Reveal Piece action that jumps the camera to a preset which shows it; selecting its graph token does the same. Camera changes never apply cube moves. A shared, integrated bridge between the two views (not a third panel) shows the current move in human language first and notation second, plus a size-aware movement count (e.g. "8 pieces move (4 corners, 4 edges)" at 3×3; corner/edge-wing/center breakdown at 4×4) — replacing the old "WHY IT MOVED" card. The affected-piece highlight is always on (no longer an opt-in toggle) and shares the same derived affected-piece set in both views, for any size.

Fractional 3D layer rotation is presentation-only. Endpoint tests compare all eighteen face/direction combinations with `applyMove` at 3×3, plus size-aware structure/randomization/live-solve/sticker-mapping invariants at 2×2 and 4×4, including sticker orientations; `cubeSurfaces`/`pieceVisible`/`camera` accept either a named preset string or a raw `{yaw, pitch}` angle (`ViewInput`), so every existing test call continues to pass raw viewpoint strings unchanged. Cube and graph use a shared animation clock with a 0.5×/1×/2× speed control; turns always animate — an explicitly-triggered, short (~1s) interaction, not ambient motion, so it is not gated behind `prefers-reduced-motion`. Mobile shows one synchronized view at a time, and the cube's `touch-action: none` keeps a drag-to-orbit gesture from fighting the page's own scroll.

Verified Randomize → accessible announcement ("N by N cube randomized using K legal moves") → Watch it solve → Play/Pause/One step/Step back/0.5×/1×/2× speed/Restart/Replay → Solved completion with the disclosure copy intact. The world remains `noindex, follow` and outside the public sitemap — indexability was not touched. No commit, push or deployment was performed.

## Deliberate scope decisions (not implemented this pass)

- **Step-by-step scramble playback** (reviewing the scramble itself move-by-move, distinct from solving) was not built as a separate mode; Randomize is instant, matching a real "shuffle" action.
- **4× playback speed** was not added; 0.5×/1×/2× covers the requested range without risking illegible animation at the fastest speed.
- **Manual per-face turning** was deliberately removed (not merely hidden) in the second feedback round — see the 2026-09-25 (feedback rounds 1–2) entry below.
